VOLUME asm-o1 | Amazing Spider-Man Omnibus Vol. 1 | Stan Lee & Steve Ditko | 1962–1966 | 43 issues
ABOUT The complete Lee/Ditko run. Everything Spider-Man is starts here — Amazing Fantasy #15 through the Master Planner saga and Ditko's exit.
TOUR The Lee and Ditko run, which is stranger, sadder and more idiosyncratic than its reputation — and much more Ditko's book than the credits box suggests.
RECEPTION Foundational | One of the small handful of Silver Age runs that is genuinely still read for pleasure rather than for history, and the consensus is that it gets better as it goes — the first ten issues are finding their feet, and by the twenties the book is extraordinary.

TOUR The best-selling comic book ever printed, and a document of the exact moment the industry decided the artist was the product.
RECEPTION Divisive/mixed | Enormously important and not, on the whole, well loved. The standard assessment is that the art is extraordinary and the writing is thin — Torment in particular gets cited for mood over substance and for lettering tics that have not aged gracefully.

VOLUME inc-o1 | Incredible Hulk Omnibus Vol. 1 | Stan Lee, Jack Kirby & Steve Ditko | 1962–1968 | 50 issues
ABOUT Where it starts. Six issues of the cancelled 1962 series, the whole Tales to Astonish run, and Hulk #102 picking the numbering back up.
TOUR The book that failed, and then quietly worked out what it was in half the page count. Six issues, forty-odd Tales to Astonish chapters, and four different artists finding four different Hulks.
RECEPTION Foundational | Universally treated as essential and rarely as anyone's favourite reading. The consensus is that the first six issues are historically fascinating and genuinely rough — the character is being invented in public — while the Tales to Astonish run is much better paced than its reputation and is where most readers say the book clicks.

ABOUT Banner on the run, hunted by a conspiracy called Home Base. A thriller more than a superhero book, and the whole Bruce Jones run in one volume.

RECEPTION Divisive/mixed | Genuinely split, and along a clean line.
